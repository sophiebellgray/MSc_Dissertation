## 📈 Overview

This dissertation investigates how **emphasis effects** in bar charts influence **speed and accuracy** in user interpretation. The research evaluates whether enhancements such as **highlighted bars or visual modifications** improve human performance in data interpretation tasks — providing a foundation for improved **data visualization design** and decision support.

> Submitted as part of the MSc Data Science program. Combines front-end data visualization (PHP/HTML/CSS) with Python-based analysis and statistical evaluation.

📄 **[View Full PDF Submission](SBG_Dissertation.pdf)**

## 🎯 Objectives

- Test the effect of **highlighting techniques** in bar charts on interpretation speed and accuracy
- Evaluate visual perception using experimental design grounded in cognitive psychology
- Provide evidence-based suggestions for visualisation best practices

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- **Graphical Literacy**  
- **Visual Encoding & Preattentive Processing**  
- **Bar Chart Perception**  
- **Statistical Hypothesis Testing (Mann-Whitney)**

## 🧪 Methodology

- Participants completed estimation tasks using custom-built bar chart visualizations
- Three chart types tested:
  - Standard bar chart (baseline)
  - Highlighted bar chart
  - Horizontal line chart
- Response **time** and **accuracy** measured and statistically compared

## 📊 Findings

- **Speed**: Highlighted bars did not significantly improve response times over standard bars
- **Accuracy**: Some marginal gains with emphasis effects, though not statistically significant
- **Implication**: More research needed, especially around population diversity and cultural interpretations of colour and shape

## 🧠 Evaluation & Impact

The dissertation bridges **cognitive psychology and data science**, offering practical insight into how visualization design affects human decision-making. While no hypothesis was confirmed with statistical significance, observed trends suggest future research potential — especially in diverse populations or with more complex chart types (e.g., scatter plots, maps).

## 🔍 Potential Applications
